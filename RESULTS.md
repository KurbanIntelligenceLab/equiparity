## 1. Piezoelectric tensor on centrosymmetric crystals

Evaluation set: 2,000 spglib-verified centrosymmetric insulators, for which the true piezoelectric
tensor is exactly zero. Both coordinate variants are evaluated for every model. False-flag fraction is
the proportion of structures whose predicted `‖e‖_F` exceeds 0.01 C/m².

| Core | Parity | false-flag (idealized) | false-flag (raw) | median ‖e‖ (idealized) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NequIP | O(3) | 0.0000 ± 0.0000 | 0.00017 ± 0.00029 | 3.19e-07 |
| NequIP | SO(3) | 0.8953 ± 0.0008 | 0.8953 ± 0.0008 | 0.666 |
| Allegro | O(3) | 0.0000 ± 0.0000 | 0.0000 ± 0.0000 | 3.84e-07 |
| Allegro | SO(3) | 0.9095 ± 0.0015 | 0.9095 ± 0.0015 | 0.960 |
| MACE | O(3) | 0.0000 ± 0.0000 | 0.00033 ± 0.00029 | 2.76e-06 |
| MACE | SO(3) | 0.9077 ± 0.0008 | 0.9077 ± 0.0008 | 0.925 |
| EquiformerV2 | SO(3) | 0.9570 ± 0.0052 | 0.9563 ± 0.0054 | 0.445 |

Across all three O(3) arms and all 3 seeds, **no structure in the idealized set exceeds the threshold**
(0 of 18,000 structure-runs). The median O(3) prediction is 3e-07 to 3e-06 C/m², at the float32 noise
level. The SO(3) medians are 0.44 to 0.96 C/m², six orders of magnitude larger.

The O(3) zero does not depend on training: it follows from the parity labelling and holds at any
parameter values, including at initialisation.

(idealized set; full 25-point tables in [A2](docs/results/a2_threshold_curves.md)):

| Core | Parity | τ=1e-4 | τ=1e-3 | τ=1e-2 | τ=1e-1 | τ=1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NequIP | O(3) | 0.0005 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| NequIP | SO(3) | 0.9170 | 0.9133 | 0.8953 | 0.8177 | 0.3548 |
| Allegro | O(3) | 0.0005 | 0.0002 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| Allegro | SO(3) | 0.9168 | 0.9155 | 0.9095 | 0.8553 | 0.4802 |
| MACE | O(3) | 0.0007 | 0.0003 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| MACE | SO(3) | 0.9168 | 0.9150 | 0.9077 | 0.8550 | 0.4672 |
| EquiformerV2 | SO(3) | 0.9968 | 0.9918 | 0.9570 | 0.8238 | 0.2203 |

Over the two decades from 1e-4 to 1e-2 the SO(3) false-flag fraction changes by 0.73 pp (Allegro),
0.92 pp (MACE), 2.17 pp (NequIP) and 3.98 pp (EquiformerV2), while the O(3) fraction remains at or
below 0.0007. The separation is therefore insensitive to the choice of threshold within this range.

### Calibration of the threshold against real tensors

The 0.01 C/m² cut is calibrated against the empirical distribution of the piezoelectric tensors the
models were trained on, rather than asserted:

| Subset | n | p5 | median | p95 | max | fraction > 0.01 C/m² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| training split | 2,649 | 0.0454 | 0.5086 | 3.7308 | 65.065 | 0.9853 |
| all labelled | 3,312 | 0.0452 | 0.5045 | 3.6512 | 65.065 | 0.9849 |

98.53 % of real piezoelectric crystals in the training split exceed 0.01 C/m². The SO(3) median
violation (0.44–0.96 C/m²) is of the same order as, and for three of four cores exceeds, the median
magnitude of a real piezoelectric tensor (0.5086 C/m²).

Pooled over 3 seeds, idealized set:

| Core | median | p95 | max |
|---|---|---|---|

symmetry.

## 4. Statistical tests

### 4.1 Structure-level (n = 2,000 paired crystals)

Wilcoxon signed-rank, paired by structure, one-sided (O(3) violation < SO(3) violation), computed
independently for each seed:

| Core | Variant | max p over seeds | fraction of crystals with O(3) < SO(3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NequIP | idealized | < 1e-300 | 0.9748 |
| NequIP | raw | < 1e-300 | 0.9800 |
| Allegro | idealized | < 1e-300 | 0.9677 |
| Allegro | raw | < 1e-300 | 0.9720 |
| MACE | idealized | < 1e-300 | 0.9623 |
| MACE | raw | < 1e-300 | 0.9673 |

The p-values underflow double precision (scipy returns 0.0); they are reported as `< 1e-300`.

### 4.2 Seed-level tests are underpowered by construction

With n = 3 seeds, a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test **cannot return p < 0.25** for any effect size.
The observed values confirm this: every piezoelectric row returns exactly 0.25 (the floor, reached when
all three seeds agree in sign), and every null row returns 0.50–1.00. These p-values are recorded in
`results/stats.json` and carry no evidential weight in either direction. Seed-level effects are
therefore reported as `Δ/σ` — the O(3)→SO(3) difference in units of the pooled per-seed standard
deviation — and no significance is claimed from them.

## 7. The raw coordinate variant

The false-flag fraction of every SO(3) arm is unchanged between the idealized and raw variants
(|Δ| ≤ 0.0007). The O(3) arms show a small nonzero fraction on the raw variant only:

| Core | Parity | ff(idealized) | ff(raw) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NequIP | O(3) | 0.0000 | 0.00017 ± 0.00029 | +0.00017 |
| Allegro | O(3) | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.00000 |
| MACE | O(3) | 0.0000 | 0.00033 ± 0.00029 | +0.00033 |
| NequIP / Allegro / MACE | SO(3) | 0.8953 / 0.9095 / 0.9077 | identical to 4 d.p. | 0.00000 |
| EquiformerV2 | SO(3) | 0.9570 | 0.9563 | −0.00067 |

Across all 9 O(3) runs, exactly **one structure** ever exceeds the threshold on the raw variant:
**mp-1227949** (index 1038), in 3 of 9 runs. Its symmetry, by spglib
([A5](docs/results/a5_ood_symmetry.md)):

| variant | sg @ 1e-5 | sg @ 1e-4 | sg @ 1e-3 | sg @ 1e-2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| idealized | 47 | 47 | 47 | 47 |
| raw | **1** (non-centrosymmetric) | **1** (non-centrosymmetric) | 47 | 47 |

The raw coordinates of this material are space group P1 at tolerances tighter than the `symprec = 1e-3`
used to select the OOD set. For those coordinates a nonzero piezoelectric tensor is not
symmetry-forbidden, and the O(3) prediction is not a symmetry violation.

This generalises. The number of raw structures spglib verifies as centrosymmetric depends on tolerance:

| symprec | idealized | raw |
|---|---|---|
| 1e-5 | 1,999 / 2,000 | 1,869 / 2,000 (93.5 %) |
| 1e-4 | 1,999 / 2,000 | 1,956 / 2,000 (97.8 %) |
| 1e-3 (selection) | 2,000 / 2,000 | 2,000 / 2,000 |

At `symprec = 1e-4`, 2.2 % of raw structures are not centrosymmetric. Independently, the O(3) false-flag
fraction on the raw variant at threshold τ = 1e-4 is 2.45–2.82 % (§2, [A2](docs/results/a2_threshold_curves.md)).
The two quantities agree in magnitude, consistent with the O(3) response on the raw set being driven by
the residual asymmetry actually present in the coordinates.

One idealized structure (mp-2923562, index 1802) also fails the tightest spglib checks; no O(3) run
assigns it a tensor above threshold.

On U₀, dipole, and elastic, |Δ/σ| ≤ 0.94 and the sign of Δ is not consistent across cores. On the
piezoelectric tensor, Δ/σ = +3.43 to +4.55 with consistent sign: the O(3) arm has lower in-distribution
error than the SO(3) arm for all three toggleable cores.

**U₀ absolute accuracy is not competitive** and is not intended to be: the target is total energy
without atomic-reference subtraction, trained on a 25,000-molecule subset with no learning-rate
schedule (METHODS §5.1). MAEs are 1.5–4.9 % of the target standard deviation (1,085.57 eV). The
comparison between arms remains valid because both share the configuration exactly.

### The Allegro U₀ difference

Allegro O(3) 31.08 ± 12.89 eV vs SO(3) 26.98 ± 4.88 eV. The difference of 4.10 eV corresponds to
Δ/σ = −0.42 (seed-level p = 0.75) and favours the SO(3) arm. The O(3) arm's own seed-to-seed standard
deviation (12.89 eV) is three times the difference. This is consistent with seed noise on an
under-converged target, and not with a parity effect. U₀ has the largest seed variance of the four
targets in every core (up to 22.50 eV for NequIP O(3)).

### The dipole result

The dipole is parity-odd, like the piezoelectric tensor, yet the two arms are within seed noise of one
another (Δ/σ = +0.42 to +0.58). QM9 contains no molecule whose point group forces the dipole to vanish,
so no test case is symmetry-forbidden and the constraint an O(3) model enforces is never active on this
dataset.

An earlier MACE dipole MAE of 0.328 D was traced to a misconfigured readout probe (the shallowest
interaction block, whose irreps are effectively scalar). With the corrected probe (METHODS §2.2) the
value is 0.0484 ± 0.0018 D. The O(3) piezoelectric zero was unaffected, as it does not depend on the
probe.

### Generalisation gap

Mean |test MAE − val MAE| / val MAE across all runs of a target: U₀ 1.5 %, dipole 0.5 %, elastic 4.8 %,
piezoelectric **17.3 %**. The piezoelectric test set has only 332 structures, and its tensor magnitudes
span three orders of magnitude, so this figure is expected to be large; it applies equally to both arms.

## 9. Size dependence of the violation metric

`‖e‖_F` is computed from a sum of per-atom (or per-edge) contributions and is therefore extensive.
Spearman ρ between violation magnitude and atom count on the idealized OOD set (median 28 atoms,
p95 96, max 444):

| Core | O(3) | SO(3) |
|---|---|---|
| NequIP | +0.501 | +0.519 |
| Allegro | +0.597 | +0.553 |
| MACE | +0.692 | +0.606 |
| EquiformerV2 | — | +0.486 |

The correlation is present in both parity modes and at similar strength, indicating it is a property of
the readout, not of the parity labelling. A fixed absolute threshold is consequently mildly
size-dependent. The O(3) result is unaffected, since its prediction is zero at any system size.

## 11. Summary of measurements

The prevalence of the distinction across released models is tabulated separately in **Table 1**
([`docs/reports/checkpoint8_prevalence_audit.md`](docs/reports/checkpoint8_prevalence_audit.md)):
of 14 surveyed architectures, 5 carry parity labels on their features, 3 are SO(3)-only, 3 are
vector-only, 3 are invariant, and 1 could not be settled by source inspection.


1. All three O(3) arms predict `‖e‖ < 0.01 C/m²` for every one of the 2,000 centrosymmetric crystals,
   in every seed, on the idealized set (median 3e-07 to 3e-06 C/m²).
2. All four SO(3) arms exceed that threshold for 89.5–95.7 % of the same crystals, with median
   magnitudes (0.44–0.96 C/m²) comparable to real piezoelectric tensors (median 0.51 C/m²).
3. The separation is stable across four decades of threshold and across both coordinate variants;
   structure-level paired tests give p < 1e-300.
4. The SO(3) violation is reproducible across seeds (ρ ≈ 0.77–0.83) and largely shared across
   architectures (Jaccard 0.93–0.99).
5. SO(3) arms have 1.002–1.398× the parameters of their O(3) counterparts.
6. On U₀, dipole, and elastic the two arms are within seed noise (|Δ/σ| ≤ 0.94); on the piezoelectric
   tensor the O(3) arm has lower in-distribution error (Δ/σ = +3.4 to +4.6).
7. The single O(3) raw-variant flag (mp-1227949) is a structure whose raw coordinates are P1 at
   tolerances below the OOD selection tolerance.
8. Retraining the SO(3) arms on 1,000 centrosymmetric crystals labelled exactly zero does not remove
   the false flags — not even on the crystals in the training set itself, where the false-flag rate
   stays at 0.895–0.922 (§12.1).
9. The crystals SO(3) does not false-flag are, with near-exactness, the ones whose rotation subgroup
   already forbids a rank-3 tensor (m-3̄m, subgroup 432): a rotational effect, not parity (§12.6).

## 12. Strengthening experiments (E1–E7)

Seven experiments run on the trained models (no headline arm was retrained; `results/stats.json` is
byte-identical). Each is summarised here with its decisive numbers and links its full appendix.
Methods are in [`METHODS.md`](METHODS.md) §8; the consolidated write-up is
[`docs/reports/checkpoint8_report.md`](docs/reports/checkpoint8_report.md).

### 12.1 Augmentation does not buy the zero (E1)

The standard objection is: *train the SO(3) model on centrosymmetric crystals labelled zero.* We
retrained the SO(3) arms (4 cores × 3 seeds) on the piezoelectric set augmented with 1,000 fresh
centrosymmetric insulators labelled exactly zero, drawn only from space groups
`{2, 12, 14, 15, 62, 225}`; validation and test partitions inherited unchanged, target scale frozen at
the un-augmented value. The evaluation splits the untouched OOD 2,000 by whether a crystal's space
group appeared in training (SEEN-SG, 1,232) or not (UNSEEN-SG, 768).

The control that decides what this means — predictions on the very crystals the model **trained on**
with zero labels:

| core | false-flag on trained zeros | median ‖e‖ on trained zeros | train MAE (zero rows) | train MAE (real rows) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NequIP | 0.8953 | 0.1200 | 0.0181 | 0.0882 |
| Allegro | 0.8990 | 0.3291 | 0.0445 | 0.1284 |
| MACE | 0.8960 | 0.0870 | 0.0140 | 0.0863 |
| EquiformerV2 | 0.9219 | 0.1153 | 0.0203 | 0.1197 |

The zero rows are fit *better* than the real-tensor rows (train MAE 0.014–0.045 vs 0.086–0.128, on
targets whose mean component magnitude is 0.145), and the model is not underfitting (train MAE on real
rows is 0.4× its test MAE). Gradient descent drives the prediction towards zero and cannot reach it.
The held-out false-flag rate barely moves — baseline → augmented, −0.000 to −0.021 on SEEN-SG and
−0.013 to −0.032 on UNSEEN-SG — while violation medians roughly halve on both subsets. The O(3) arms,
not retrained, remain at 0.0000. Augmentation does improve non-centrosymmetric test MAE for every core
(e.g. EquiformerV2 0.2157 → 0.1774): better regressors that still predict impossible values. This
outcome fell outside both anticipated results, so it carries an
[off-cycle report](docs/reports/checkpoint8_offcycle_e1.md).
Full table: [E1](docs/results/e1_augmentation.md).

### 12.2 The symmetry-breaking curve (E2)

Displacing a centrosymmetric parent along a [001] polar mode, `x(δ) = x_parent + δ·Δx`, turns the
symmetry off continuously. The parent's *rotation* subgroup decides whether the experiment can show a
parity effect at all: cubic perovskites (Pm-3̄m, subgroup 432) forbid a rank-3 tensor by rotation
alone, so both arms start at machine zero there. **Rutile TiO₂** (P4₂/mnm 136 → P4₂nm 102, subgroup
422) permits a rank-3 invariant, so only parity forbids a response. On TiO₂ at δ=0 the O(3) arms give
1.9e-07–9.1e-07 while the SO(3) arms give 0.092–0.282 (seed-averaged per core) — a spurious floor
comparable to the SO(3) response at δ=0.05, so it dominates the physical signal throughout the small-δ
regime. Every frame was spglib-verified at symprec 1e-8. Full curves:
[E2](docs/results/e2_symmetry_breaking.md).

### 12.3 The parity guarantee is differentiable (E3)

For an O(3) model `T(I·x) = −T(x)`, so at a centrosymmetric geometry the Jacobian `J = dT/dr` satisfies
`J∘P = −J`: the learned response to symmetry breaking lives entirely on inversion-odd (polar) modes.
Measured on 20 crystals across 19 space groups, both arms of the three e3nn cores, the even-subspace
energy fraction `‖J·P_even‖/‖J‖` is O(3) **1.4e-07–9.7e-07** vs SO(3) **0.42–0.54** — five to six
orders of magnitude, no overlap over any structure or seed. Every Jacobian is certified against finite
differences (median rel err 6.6e-05–1.4e-04). EquiformerV2 is excluded: its vendored code detaches the
Wigner-D matrices, so autograd yields only the radial part of `dT/dr` (finite differences disagree by
45%, versus 9e-4 on NequIP). Full table: [E3](docs/results/e3_jacobian.md).

### 12.4 Test-time inversion averaging (E4)

The second proposed fix is to symmetrise the output, `T_sym = [T(x) − T(I·x)]/2`. On a centrosymmetric
crystal `I·x` is the same periodic structure up to a permutation, so any permutation-invariant model
gives `T(I·x) = T(x)` (measured to 1e-6) and `T_sym ≡ 0` identically — on **both** OOD variants, not
only the idealized one. The SO(3) false-flag rate collapses ~0.90 → ~0.000, but for a reason carrying
no parity information, and the fix presupposes knowing the target's parity in advance — precisely what
O(3) features encode. EquiformerV2 is the exception (0.95 → 0.82): it violates even the identity the
fix relies on. Full table: [E4](docs/results/e4_inversion_averaging.md).

### 12.5 Output-level equivariance audit (E5)

Applying an improper operation `M` to the input, the O(3) arms reproduce `D(M) T(x)` to ~6e-7; the
SO(3) arms violate it by O(1) — the parity defect measured directly at the output. All three e3nn cores
satisfy the *rotation* law in both arms (~1e-6) and are bit-deterministic. EquiformerV2 fails both: its
rotation-law error is 7e-2–1.1e-1, and it is the only nondeterministic model (a random per-edge
reference frame is drawn on every forward pass; §12 discussion). Full table:
[E5](docs/results/e5_output_parity.md).

### 12.6 Named materials, and where SO(3)'s zeros come from (E6, E7)

For ten familiar centrosymmetric compounds the O(3) prediction is machine zero throughout. The
non-cubic ones are where parity is the only thing standing between the model and an impossible value:
for **corundum Al₂O₃ (sapphire)** the SO(3) arms predict 1.13 (NequIP), 0.72 (Allegro), 0.84 (MACE),
0.21 (EquiformerV2). The cubic m-3̄m compounds (NaCl, diamond, Si, MgO, CaF₂, CsCl, SrTiO₃) are
near-zero for the exact-SO(3) cores too — and E7 explains why. Splitting the OOD set by point group,
the three e3nn cores false-flag **exactly 0.000** on all 166 m-3̄m crystals (rotation subgroup 432
forbids rank-3) and 0.889–1.000 on the 18 m-3̄ crystals (subgroup 23 permits it). 1,834 of 2,000
crystals (91.7%) lie outside what rotation can enforce, close to the observed SO(3) false-flag rate.
**SO(3)'s zeros are the ones rotations already guarantee; O(3)'s are the strictly larger set that
parity guarantees.** This is scoped as supplementary and does not change the headline framing of the
~9%. Full tables: [E6](docs/results/e6_named_materials.md),
[E7](docs/results/e7_rotation_subgroup.md).

### 12.7 Prevalence, and the extensivity check (Table 1, F2)

Across 14 released architectures classified by inspecting source (Table 1,
[prevalence audit](docs/reports/checkpoint8_prevalence_audit.md)), 5 carry parity labels, 3 are
SO(3)-only (including EquiformerV2 and the eSCN family), 3 are vector-only, 3 are invariant, and 1 is
undetermined. Separately, because ‖e‖ is extensive (§9), the headline false-flag rate was recomputed
with the size-normalised metric `‖e‖ / n_atoms` against a threshold rescaled by the median cell: every
O(3) arm stays at exactly 0.0000 and the SO(3) arms move by at most 0.0177, so the conclusion does not
depend on size normalisation ([F2](docs/results/f2_per_atom.md)).

## 13. Discussion

The measurements are consistent with the mechanism described in METHODS §1: parity labelling makes the
symmetry-mandated zero structural, and its removal does not merely degrade the prediction but permits a
physically impossible one.

Two results delimit the scope of that statement. First, on U₀ and the elastic tensor — both parity-even
— the arms are indistinguishable, as expected, since no odd-parity constraint is active. Second, and
less obviously, the same is true of the **dipole**, which is parity-odd. QM9 contains no molecule whose
symmetry forces the dipole to vanish, so the constraint is never binding and a model unable to represent
it is never tested against it. The dipole therefore separates two properties that the piezoelectric
experiment confounds: being an odd-parity target, and being evaluated where symmetry forbids a nonzero
value. Only the second produces a difference between the arms.

That the violation is shared across architectures (§5) locates it in the symmetry class of the input
rather than in any particular model, and the parameter counts (§6) exclude capacity as an explanation.
Three of the strengthening experiments sharpen the mechanism. The rotation-subgroup analysis (§12.6)
shows that the crystals SO(3) gets right are precisely those where *rotation alone* already forbids a
rank-3 tensor — so parity is doing exactly the additional work the mechanism predicts, no more and no
less. The Jacobian analysis (§12.3) shows the O(3) guarantee is not merely a zero but a differentiable
one, supported entirely on polar modes. And the two proposed remedies fail in instructive ways: data
augmentation cannot drive even a trained-on zero below threshold (§12.1), and output symmetrisation
succeeds only by assuming the parity knowledge that O(3) features already carry (§12.4).

EquiformerV2 is a deployed model, not a research ablation, and it exhibits the highest false-flag
fraction measured here (0.957). Its inclusion indicates that the behaviour is not an artefact of the
matched-pair construction used for the other three cores.

**EquiformerV2's evaluation is stochastic.** Its forward pass draws a fresh random per-edge reference
frame on every call (`models/equiformer_v2/edge_rot_mat.py`), so it is nondeterministic even in
`eval()` mode. Over five seeded draws of the full 2,000-crystal OOD evaluation the median violation has
a relative standard deviation of 6.5e-03 and the false-flag fraction a standard deviation of 5.0e-04;
the value reported above is a single draw and lies within that spread. No conclusion here depends on
it. The nondeterminism is itself a measurement: an exactly SO(3)-equivariant network's output does not
depend on the arbitrary frame, so the spread is a direct read-out of EquiformerV2's rotational
equivariance error — confirmed independently in [E5](docs/results/e5_output_parity.md), where it fails
the rotation law by 7e-02 to 1.1e-01 while every e3nn core satisfies it to ~1e-06. All EquiformerV2
figures in the supplementary experiments are means over five seeded draws.

## 14. Limitations

1. **All three O(3) arms share the e3nn irrep implementation.** The intended non-e3nn O(3) control
   (CliffordSTF) was withdrawn for numerical conditioning reasons (METHODS §7). The study therefore
   cannot exclude that some part of the effect is specific to e3nn's realisation of O(3)-equivariance,
   although EquiformerV2 — which does not use parity-labelled irreps — provides partial evidence
   against this on the SO(3) side.
2. **U₀ is far from converged** and its absolute accuracy is not comparable to the QM9 literature
   (METHODS §5.1). With n = 3 seeds the arms show **no consistent direction across cores** (the sign of
   Δ differs by core and seed variance is large); this is not a tested null, and no null is claimed.
3. **Seed-level statistics are underpowered** (n = 3; Wilcoxon p ≥ 0.25 by construction). Only the
   structure-level tests carry statistical weight.
4. **The violation metric is extensive** (§9), so absolute magnitudes depend on system size.
5. **Timing is not comparable across cores**, which ran on two different GPU classes (§10), and the
   monetary cost was not recorded.
6. **The piezoelectric test set is small** (332 structures) with a 17.3 % val→test MAE gap.
7. **The raw OOD variant is only centrosymmetric at the tolerance it was selected with** (§7); results
   on it should be read together with the symmetry audit in [A5](docs/results/a5_ood_symmetry.md).
8. Hyperparameters were not tuned per architecture; all cores share layers, `r_max`, and feature width.
9. **The augmentation experiment (§12.1) used one budget and one schedule** — 1,000 zero-labelled
   crystals (~27 % of the training set), 150 epochs, no loss up-weighting of the zero rows. It shows
   that under matched hyperparameters SO(3) still false-flags ~90 % of the crystals it was trained to
   call zero; it does not establish that the rate cannot be forced lower under a more aggressive
   regime.
10. **The SEEN/UNSEEN subsets of §12.1 differ in composition, not only in space group** — median ‖e‖
    is already higher on SEEN-SG in the baseline runs — so only the within-subset change relative to
    baseline is interpretable, not the SEEN-vs-UNSEEN difference itself.
