\title[Parity-blind equivariant networks predict impossible properties]{Rotation is not enough: parity-blind equivariant networks predict physically impossible material properties}

\abstract{Equivariant neural networks are the standard architecture for predicting molecular and materials properties, and are routinely described as E(3)-equivariant. Many widely deployed models, however, are equivariant only to rotations, not to reflections: their internal features carry no parity labels. We show that this seemingly technical distinction decides whether a model can make physically impossible predictions. Symmetry forces the piezoelectric tensor of a centrosymmetric crystal to vanish identically, providing a label-free test of physical validity. Across matched pairs of three architectures that differ only in parity labelling, models with parity labels predict zero for all 2,000 symmetry-verified centrosymmetric crystals, whereas their rotation-only counterparts predict substantial tensors for 90--96\% of them, with magnitudes typical of real piezoelectrics. Retraining on zero-labelled crystals, loss reweighting, and test-time symmetrisation all fail to restore the exact zero. Of 15 released architectures audited, nine cannot structurally forbid such predictions. Feature parity, not training, determines whether symmetry-forbidden outputs occur.}

\keywords{equivariant neural networks, parity, O(3) versus SO(3) equivariance, piezoelectric tensor, centrosymmetric crystals, Neumann's principle, machine learning for materials}
