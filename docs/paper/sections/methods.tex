\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Physical setting and test statistic}

Neumann's principle requires every macroscopic property tensor of a crystal to be invariant under all operations of the crystal's point group. The piezoelectric tensor $e_{ijk}$, relating polarisation to strain, is a parity-odd rank-3 Cartesian tensor: under spatial inversion it transforms as $e \mapsto (-1)^{3} e = -e$. For a centrosymmetric crystal, whose space group contains the inversion, invariance therefore forces $e = 0$ identically. Of the 230 crystallographic space groups, 92 are centrosymmetric. Because the identity is exact, it supplies a test statistic requiring no reference labels: for each evaluated structure we compute the violation magnitude $\lVert e \rVert_F$, the Frobenius norm of the predicted tensor, which equals the prediction error exactly. The change of basis between Cartesian components and irreducible representations is orthonormal, so the norm is identical in either basis. The false-flag fraction at threshold $\tau$ is the fraction of evaluated structures with $\lVert e \rVert_F > \tau$. The operating point $\tau = 0.01$\,C\,m$^{-2}$ is calibrated against the empirical distribution of real piezoelectric tensors in the training data rather than asserted, and all results are reported alongside the full curve over 25 log-spaced thresholds spanning $10^{-4}$ to $1$\,C\,m$^{-2}$. Because the readout sums per-atom or per-edge contributions, the violation magnitude is extensive (Spearman correlation with atom count 0.49--0.69 in both parity modes); a per-atom variant of the metric with a correspondingly rescaled threshold leaves all conclusions unchanged (Supplementary Note~3).

\subsection*{Architectures and the parity toggle}

Four cores are evaluated. NequIP, Allegro, and MACE are built as matched pairs sharing one architecture, one set of hyperparameters, one data pipeline, and one set of seeds, differing only in the parity labelling of their features. EquiformerV2 is included unmodified as a deployed model that is rotation-only by construction.

All matched arms are generated by a single helper that emits the feature irreducible representations for each angular degree $l$ up to $l_{\max}$. The O(3) arm assigns the natural spherical-harmonic parity $(-1)^{l}$, giving the pattern $0e, 1o, 2e, \dots$; the SO(3) arm declares every degree parity-even, $0e, 1e, 2e, \dots$. Both the edge spherical harmonics and the hidden features are relabelled, so the geometric content of the two arms is identical and only the transformation law that the feature algebra enforces differs. The framework-specific construction of the SO(3) arms --- and why one framework's preset \texttt{parity} boolean does not itself serve as the toggle --- is detailed in Supplementary Note~2. EquiformerV2's embeddings are indexed by degree and order only and carry no parity label anywhere; it therefore has no O(3) arm, which is the reason for its inclusion.

Each target is expressed in irreducible representations of O(3): the internal energy $U_0$ as one even scalar; the molecular dipole as one odd vector, read out directly by an equivariant degree-1 linear head, never assembled from predicted charges and positions; the elasticity tensor as the 21 even components $2\times0e \oplus 2\times2e \oplus 1\times4e$; and the piezoelectric tensor as the 18 odd components $2\times1o \oplus 1\times2o \oplus 1\times3o$. In the SO(3) arm the output head is relabelled with every odd representation declared even --- precisely the step that permits an SO(3) model to emit a symmetry-forbidden odd tensor. Index symmetries of the rank-3 and rank-4 tensors are encoded through reduced tensor products, and the Cartesian--irreducible change of basis is orthonormal. Each core reads out from the deepest layer whose features still carry degree $l>0$ and sums per-atom (or, for Allegro, per-edge) contributions per structure, which preserves equivariance; EquiformerV2's coefficients are remapped from its degree-major layout to the multiplicity-major layout of the matched cores.

\subsection*{Parity verification gate}

Because a mislabelled arm would invalidate the study, the toggle was verified numerically before any training. For each matched core and arm, an internal equivariant feature layer was probed with a random proper rotation and a random improper operation, measuring the maximal deviation between features of the transformed input and the correspondingly transformed features. O(3) arms must satisfy both laws; SO(3) arms must satisfy the rotation law and fail the reflection law. All six arms behaved as required. EquiformerV2, having no parity-labelled features to probe, is verified at the output level by the evaluation itself and by the output-level audit described below. The per-core rotation and reflection errors, the single-precision floor that applies to MACE, and the positive control confirming the probe discriminates are given in Supplementary Note~2 and Supplementary Table~2.

\subsection*{Datasets and splits}

QM9\cite{ramakrishnan2014qm9} supplies the two molecular targets. The 3,054 uncharacterised molecules are excluded, leaving 130,831 structures, split randomly (seed 42) into 110,000 training, 10,000 validation, and 10,831 test molecules. $U_0$ is the total internal energy at 0\,K converted to eV, deliberately without atomic-reference subtraction (see Training protocol). The dipole label is constructed from the reference Mulliken charges as $\sum_i q_i \mathbf{r}_i$ and converted to debye; such a dipole underestimates the density-functional magnitude by roughly 10--25\%, but its direction and parity are exact, which is what the comparison requires, and the bias applies identically to both arms.

Crystal targets come from the Materials Project (retrieved 2026-07-04; CC-BY-4.0), accessed and processed with pymatgen\cite{ong2013pymatgen}. The piezoelectric dataset\cite{dejong2015piezo} comprises 3,312 structures with density-functional perturbation-theory tensors after dropping four outliers with $\lVert e \rVert > 50$\,C\,m$^{-2}$, split 2,649/331/332; the elasticity dataset comprises 13,080 structures after dropping 147 failed calculations with components beyond 2,000\,GPa, split 10,464/1,308/1,308 (80/10/10, seed 42).

The centrosymmetric evaluation population was assembled by querying the Materials Project for insulators (band gap above 0.1\,eV, since piezoelectricity is defined for insulators) in the 92 centrosymmetric space groups, shuffling with seed 42, re-verifying every candidate's space group with spglib at tolerance $10^{-3}$, and keeping the first 2,000 confirmed structures; 103 candidates failed re-verification and were rejected. No labels are stored --- the true tensor is exactly zero by symmetry. Every model is evaluated on two coordinate variants of the same 2,000 materials: an idealized variant with coordinates snapped onto the exact space group, which tests the structural guarantee, and a raw variant with the unmodified density-functional-relaxed coordinates, which tests behaviour on real data whose coordinates satisfy inversion only to within a tolerance. The interaction between the selection tolerance and the raw variant is quantified in Supplementary Note~4.

\subsection*{Training protocol}

Both arms of every matched pair train identically: Adam\cite{kingma2015adam} with learning rate $2\times10^{-3}$, no weight decay, no learning-rate schedule, mean-squared-error loss on normalised targets, three interaction layers, 5.0\,\AA{} cutoff, 64 features, float32 weights with float64 geometry, no early stopping. Per-target settings: $U_0$ and dipole use $l_{\max}=2$, 100 epochs, batch 32, and a 25,000-molecule training subset; elasticity uses $l_{\max}=2$, 120 epochs, batch 16, full training split; the piezoelectric tensor uses $l_{\max}=3$, 150 epochs, batch 16, full training split. Scalar targets are standardised by the training mean and standard deviation; vector and tensor targets are scaled by the training standard deviation only, because subtracting a mean would add a constant non-equivariant offset and destroy the property under test. Predictions are rescaled to physical units before any metric is computed. Metrics are reported at the final epoch.

$U_0$ serves as a parity-even control, not as a competitive benchmark: the target is total energy spanning roughly 18,000\,eV with standard deviation 1,086\,eV, trained on a subset without a schedule, so absolute errors are orders of magnitude above tuned literature models. Both arms share this configuration exactly, which is what the comparison requires; $U_0$ also has the largest seed variance of the four targets, which limits the resolution of its between-arm comparison.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis}

Structure-level comparisons use one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests paired by structure over the 2,000 evaluated crystals, computed independently for each seed, testing whether the O(3) violation is smaller than the SO(3) violation. With three seeds, seed-level signed-rank tests cannot return $p<0.25$ for any effect size; seed-level effects are therefore reported as the between-arm difference in units of the pooled per-seed standard deviation, and no significance is claimed from them. Reproducibility across seeds is quantified by Spearman rank correlation between per-structure violation vectors of independently seeded runs, and agreement between architectures by the same correlation together with the Jaccard index of the flagged sets, computed per matched seed and averaged.

\subsection*{Augmentation and loss-weight experiments}

The augmentation experiment retrains only the SO(3) arms (four cores, three seeds) on the piezoelectric training split plus 1,000 additional spglib-verified centrosymmetric insulators labelled with the exact zero tensor, drawn from the six most populous centrosymmetric space groups (numbers 2, 12, 14, 15, 62, and 225), idealized identically to the evaluation population, and verified disjoint from both the evaluation population and the original training identifiers. Validation and test partitions are inherited unchanged, and the target normalisation is frozen at its unaugmented value so that violation magnitudes remain comparable. The O(3) arms are not retrained: their zeros are structural and hold for any training data. Evaluation partitions the untouched population by whether each crystal's space group appeared in the augmentation list (1,232 seen versus 768 unseen), and an in-training control reports predictions on the zero-labelled crystals themselves, distinguishing zeros that were never learned from learned zeros that fail to generalise. The loss-weight sweep repeats the NequIP configuration with the 1,016 exactly-zero rows (1,000 injected plus 16 real) up-weighted in the mean-squared-error loss by factors of 10 and 100, three seeds each, all other settings identical.

\subsection*{Symmetry-breaking sweep, Jacobian analysis, and output-level audits}

The symmetry-breaking sweep displaces a centrosymmetric parent along a [001] polar mode, $x(\delta) = x_{\mathrm{parent}} + \delta\,\Delta x$, over 33 amplitudes per material, with every frame verified by spglib at tolerance $10^{-8}$; at the looser selection tolerance, frames below $\delta\approx0.006$ are wrongly reported centrosymmetric because the maximal displacement falls under the tolerance --- the same phenomenon as the raw-variant artifact of Supplementary Note~4. Rutile TiO$_2$ (space group 136 distorting to 102) carries the argument because its proper-rotation subgroup 422 admits a rank-3 invariant; the perovskite panels (parent subgroup 432, admitting none) are reported with that caveat. Both subgroup statements are verified by Reynolds-operator rank tests in the released test suite rather than asserted.

The Jacobian analysis computes $J = \partial T/\partial \mathbf{r}$ by automatic differentiation at the centrosymmetric geometry of 20 evaluated crystals spanning 19 space groups, for both arms of the three matched cores and all seeds, at fixed graph connectivity. The primary statistic is the even-subspace energy fraction $\lVert J P_{\mathrm{even}}\rVert_F / \lVert J \rVert_F$ with $P_{\mathrm{even}} = (\mathrm{id} + P)/2$, where $P$ permutes atoms by the structure's inversion operation and flips displacement signs; the identity $J \circ P = -J$ makes the fraction exactly zero for an O(3) model. Parity scores of leading singular vectors are reported only as a secondary check, because a trained SO(3) model is approximately odd and its scores do not separate the arms cleanly. Every Jacobian is certified against central finite differences before use.

The output-level audit applies, for every trained piezoelectric model, a mirror test comparing $T(Mx)$ against the parity-transformed $D(M)\,T(x)$ for a random improper operation $M$, a rotation test with a proper operation (which both arms must satisfy), and a determinism test of repeated forward passes, with the relative laws measured on non-centrosymmetric structures where the prediction is nonzero. Test-time inversion averaging evaluates $T_{\mathrm{sym}}(x) = [T(x) - T(I\!\cdot\!x)]/2$ on both coordinate variants, where $I\!\cdot\!x$ inverts positions about the cell origin; on an exactly centrosymmetric input the inverted structure is the same periodic crystal up to a permutation, making the construction identically zero for any permutation- and translation-invariant model, a property verified to $10^{-6}$ and central to interpreting that experiment.

\subsection*{EquiformerV2 provenance and stochastic evaluation}

All EquiformerV2 findings were reproduced against the pinned upstream source, to establish that they are properties of the released code rather than of our packaging; the file-by-file comparison against upstream, the auditable shim for the one inherited method, and the reruns that match the vendored results are given in Supplementary Note~10 and Supplementary Tables~15--16. Two properties of the released code bear directly on how EquiformerV2 is evaluated here. First, because the forward pass draws a fresh random reference frame per edge on every call, EquiformerV2 is nondeterministic even in evaluation mode; every EquiformerV2 value in the strengthening experiments is therefore a mean over five seeded evaluation draws, with its spread reported in Supplementary Note~10. Second, its autograd Jacobian omits the angular dependence (the Wigner matrices are detached from positions), disagreeing with finite differences by tens of percent, which is the reason for its exclusion from the Jacobian analysis.

\subsection*{Reproducibility}

Every run records a provenance manifest: git revision and cleanliness, configuration hash, dataset and split manifests, seed, package versions, host, GPU model, CUDA and driver versions. Final-result runs refuse to execute on a modified source tree. Seeding fixes the Python hash seed, the standard random module, NumPy, and torch including CUDA, with deterministic algorithms enabled and benchmark autotuning disabled. Raw datasets are external and referenced by manifests with content hashes; splits are generated once and referenced by identifier in every result. The full software environment --- including the two mutually exclusive e3nn installation profiles required by the conflicting framework versions and the exact package pins --- is given in Supplementary Note~6, and the compute budget and per-core timing in Supplementary Table~9.
