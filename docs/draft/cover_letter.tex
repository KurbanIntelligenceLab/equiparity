\textbf{To the Editorial Office, \\ \textit{Nature Communications}}

Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, ``\textbf{Parity-blind equivariant networks predict physically impossible material properties},'' for consideration as an Article in \textit{Nature Communications}.

Whether physical symmetry should be built into atomistic neural networks or learned from data has been argued almost entirely over rotations, and in the currency of accuracy, where the case for learning is real. Our manuscript opens a second axis, on which the currency is validity. Many widely deployed models described as ``E(3)-equivariant'' are equivariant only to rotations: their features carry no parity labels. Neumann's principle forces the piezoelectric tensor of every centrosymmetric crystal to vanish exactly, so on our population of 2,000 verified centrosymmetric insulators any nonzero prediction is a certified error, obtained without labels and without a reference calculation.

Against this test, feature parity alone decides the outcome. Across matched pairs of three architectures whose sole intended difference is the parity labelling of their features, the parity-labelled arms return zero at the floating-point floor on every crystal, while the rotation-only arms predict tensors of ordinary piezoelectric magnitude on 90--96\% of them, in exact agreement with a group-theoretic account, proved in the Supplementary Information, that identifies in advance the 8.3\% of crystals rotation alone still protects; unmodified EquiformerV2 reproduces the behaviour in a deployed model we never touched. None of the training strategies we tested removes the failure: retraining on a thousand crystals labelled with the exact zero, and up-weighting those rows a hundredfold, still leaves two-thirds of them flagged, evidence that the tested strategies do not recover a structural guarantee where the symmetry is exact. An audit of eighteen released architectures finds that only half of the twelve able to produce a rank-3 tensor output type parity in their features, and the rotation-only class includes the current generation of universal potentials: the obstruction sits in a symmetry class in active deployment, not in any single model.

The work sits at the journal's intersection of machine learning and the physical sciences, and offers its readership a label-free validity test for equivariant models, a mechanism-level account of why parity-blind networks violate a symmetry they are advertised to respect, and an actionable criterion for architecture choice: a live debate addressed with a measurement rather than an opinion. The manuscript conforms to the Article format, with a main text under 5,000 words, five display items, and 55 references.

All code, dataset manifests, splits, and the machine-readable statistics behind every figure and table are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/KurbanIntelligenceLab/equiparity} under the MIT licence. Every dataset used is public: crystal data from the Materials Project (CC BY 4.0), including the de Jong et al.\ piezoelectric database, and molecular data from QM9 (CC0).

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously or posted to a preprint server, and is not under consideration elsewhere; we have no related manuscripts under consideration or in press. All authors have approved the submission, and we declare no competing interests. We have no reviewer suggestions and no exclusion requests. Thank you for your consideration.
