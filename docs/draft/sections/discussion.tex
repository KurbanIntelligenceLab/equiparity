\section*{Discussion}

The measurements confirm a single mechanism, and the theory names it. Parity labels make a symmetry-mandated zero structural: Theorem~\ref{thm:zero} holds at any parameter values, so the zero cannot be trained away because it was never trained in, and the Jacobian identity extends the certainty to derivatives. Removing the labels converts that certainty into an ordinary regression error. A rotation-only model retains precisely the rotational part of the constraint, up to the ceiling Corollary~\ref{cor:rot} fixes in advance, and the agreement between architecturally unrelated models places the effect in the symmetry class rather than in any network.

This bears on whether symmetry can be learned from data rather than built in. On rotations the evidence that it can is real~\cite{brehmer2024scale,bigi2026unconstrained}; our augmentation experiment poses the same proposition where the symmetry is exact, and every training strategy we tested fails it. An SO(3) network can represent the zero tensor and can be fitted arbitrarily close to it, but nothing in its construction enforces the exact zero at every parameter value: the guarantee is not structural, and the strategies tested here did not supply it. The distinction that matters is therefore not equivariant versus unconstrained, nor how much data is available, but whether the predicted quantity is an approximation or an exact identity: where the physics permits any value learned symmetry is a reasonable trade, and where it permits exactly one an approximate constraint delivers an error the size of the property. Non-conservative force models made the same bargain against energy conservation, and were found wanting when assessed against the physics rather than the test error~\cite{bigi2025forces}.

The result also gives quantitative content to a distinction the field treats as terminology. Curie's principle~\cite{curie1894symmetry}, that an equivariant map cannot produce outputs less symmetric than its inputs, is the foundation on which equivariant networks are advertised. For improper operations it is silently violated by a widely deployed subclass of the models it is invoked to describe: upholding it for tensor properties requires the full orthogonal group, and rotational equivariance upholds only the proper half.

The mechanism also dictates where the guarantee should live. Space-group-informed modules, canonicalization and symmetry-adapted output decompositions repair the output of a model whose features remain parity-blind, and the vacuity of test-time antisymmetrization makes the cost concrete: such a repair covers only the quantity someone remembered to repair, takes the target's parity as an input, and leaves everything else on the same features unprotected. Feature-level parity instead makes the guarantee available to every quantity the features feed, derivatives included, provided the readout declares the target's parity correctly. This is not an argument against readout-level methods, but an argument that the feature symmetry group is a prior design decision, made once, made invisibly, and inherited by every downstream head.

The practical rule is narrow enough to check before a model is built. Parity labels are required whenever the target is parity-odd and the evaluation population may contain structures whose point group forbids it. Corollary~\ref{cor:rot} makes the second condition precise: rotation alone suffices only where the proper-rotation subgroup annihilates the target, for a rank-3 tensor class m$\bar 3$m alone, 8.3\% of the crystals evaluated here. Nor is the rule confined to piezoelectricity. In the electric-dipole approximation the second-harmonic-generation tensor shares the same rank and parity, and the same identity in every centrosymmetric crystal; it is an active target for machine-learned discovery of nonlinear-optical materials~\cite{optixnet2026}, where a rotation-only backbone is unconstrained for the same reason.

A remaining objection is that a screening pipeline can filter centrosymmetric candidates out first, as high-throughput nonlinear-optical searches routinely do. Pre-filtering is the same repair with the same costs, inheriting every weakness of the symmetry determination that drives it, measurably so in our own data: all 2,000 evaluation crystals are centrosymmetric at the selection tolerance, but 2.2\% are not at $10^{-4}$ and 6.5\% are not at $10^{-5}$, and structures from relaxation or generative models carry no reliable space-group label. A parity-labelled model needs no filter and behaves correctly at the boundary: the single raw-coordinate flag in our data proved genuinely non-centrosymmetric. This also distinguishes these violations from the minute responses sensitive resonance techniques detect in nominally centrosymmetric crystals~\cite{aktas2021nominally}, whose samples truly break inversion; the SO(3) violations occur on exactly symmetric structures at full physical magnitude.

The study has limits: EqV2 is unmodified and reported strictly as measured, so no conclusion depends on it (Methods). All three matched O(3) arms use e3nn~\cite{geiger2022e3nn} and an intended non-e3nn control was withdrawn. A second non-e3nn implementation, tested as a random-init structural control rather than as a trained fourth arm, reproduces the structural zero on periodic centrosymmetric crystals without that core's amplification failure (\snote{snote:hotpp}), which argues against an e3nn-specific origin for the effect; a trained, e3nn-independent arm on the full population was not run, so an implementation-specific contribution to the trained accuracy comparisons cannot be excluded. Three seeds limit seed-level statistics, so statistical weight rests on the structure-level paired tests. The readout sums per-atom or per-edge contributions, so the predicted tensors are extensive where the piezoelectric and elastic properties are intensive. Both consequences of that choice were measured rather than argued: a mean-pooled readout is size-invariant where the summed one is not, and retraining the full piezoelectric grid mean-pooled leaves the O(3) false-flag fraction at exactly zero and shifts the SO(3) fraction by less than the seed spread, so the separation between the two feature symmetry groups is a property of the parity labelling rather than of extensive pooling. The scalar-energy control is deliberately unconverged, and hyperparameters were shared rather than tuned. The loss-weight sweep used one architecture, and neither it nor the zero-injection sweep excludes that more aggressive regimes push violations lower. The prevalence audit is pinned to the versions recorded in the Supplementary Information.

Whether a machine-learned model can make physically impossible tensor predictions is therefore decided before training begins, by whether its features carry parity labels, a property most released architectures lack and none advertises. Because it is not advertised it cannot be checked: a model card reporting a maximum angular degree but not a feature symmetry group withholds the fact that decides whether the model can respect the improper half of Neumann's principle. For parity-odd targets, and for any pipeline that may one day attach one, the label is not bookkeeping; it is the physics.
