\title[Not all symmetry can be learned]{Not all symmetry can be learned: parity-blind equivariant networks predict physically impossible material properties}

\abstract{Equivariant neural networks are the standard architecture for predicting molecular and materials properties, and are routinely described as E(3)-equivariant. Many widely deployed models, however, are equivariant only to rotations, not to reflections: their internal features carry no parity labels. Whether symmetry should be hard-wired or learned from data is an active debate, but it has been argued largely on rotations and in the currency of accuracy. On the reflection axis the currency is different: symmetry forces the piezoelectric tensor of a centrosymmetric crystal to vanish identically, so a nonzero prediction is not approximately wrong but physically impossible. Across matched pairs of three architectures differing only in parity labelling, models with parity labels stay below the detection threshold on all 2,000 symmetry-verified centrosymmetric crystals, at a median of $3\times10^{-7}$\,C\,m$^{-2}$, whereas their rotation-only counterparts exceed it for 90--96\% of them, at magnitudes typical of real piezoelectrics. Data does not substitute for the constraint: training on the exact answer at a hundredfold loss weight still leaves two-thirds of those crystals flagged. Of 15 released architectures, only six type parity in their features, and three carry no parity information at all. Feature parity, not training, decides whether symmetry-forbidden outputs occur.}
\keywords{equivariant neural networks, inductive bias, learned versus built-in symmetry, parity, O(3) versus SO(3) equivariance, piezoelectric tensor, centrosymmetric crystals, Neumann's principle}
